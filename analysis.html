📑 Key Findings for the Research Paper

Synthesizing empirical results from 500,000 respondents across 10 exploratory visualizations:

  • Evidence for Time Displacement (Hypothesis 1 Supported): High social media usage (> 5.0 hrs/day) displaces 2.54 hours per week of study compared to low usage (≤ 3.0 hrs/day), confirmed by parametric regression modeling (β = -0.74, p < 0.0001).
  • Evidence for Attentional Fragmentation (Hypothesis 2 Supported): Students log a median of 9.73 discrete app sessions daily; students with ≥ 17 daily sessions exhibit 42% shorter sustained attention spans (dropping from 56 to 32 minutes).
  • Productivity Cliff at 5.0 Hours: Academic productivity remains robust across low-to-moderate usage (9.31 down to 8.67), but drops precipitously to 7.72 for heavy users, demonstrating a non-linear threshold effect.
  • Cognitive Fatigue Cascade: Digital addiction score (r = -0.62 with attention span) and brain rot index (r = -0.66 with productivity) act as primary mediating mechanisms connecting excessive screen time to academic decline.
  • Mobile Device Vulnerability: Over 60.6% of students rely exclusively on smartphones for digital access, creating continuous proximity to algorithmic notifications during active lecture and study hours.

SQL Relational Data Analysis

The cleaned global student behavior dataset (N = 500,000 across 50 nations) was structured into two relational tables: Students and Countries. Using Structured Query Language (SQL), we perform relational modeling, subgroup filtering, cohort grouping, and aggregate evaluations to analyze behavioral distributions, assess distraction dynamics, and test our research hypotheses.

🗄️ Relational Database Schema: Two Related Tables

Our database architecture organizes the 500,000 survey records into two relational tables:

1. Students

Main entity table storing individual student digital behavior (500,000 records):

  • student_id (Primary Key)
  • country_id (Foreign Key referencing Countries)
  • age (15–25)
  • gender (Female, Male, Other)
  • device_access (Smartphone, Laptop, Shared, Both)
  • social_media_hours (0.00–9.00 hrs/day)
  • study_hours_per_week (2.00–43.32 hrs/wk)
  • productivity_score (1.00–10.00)
  • digital_addiction_score (0.00–40.22)
  • brain_rot_index (0.00–59.00)
  • academic_risk_score (0.00–24.07)
  • usage_level (Low, Moderate, High)

2. Countries

Lookup dimension table storing national socio-economic indicators (50 countries):

  • country_id (Primary Key)
  • country_name (50 surveyed sovereign nations)
  • development_level (Developed, Developing, Underdeveloped)
  • poverty_rate_percent (National poverty index)
  • internet_infrastructure_index (0–100 infrastructure rating)

🔗 Table Relationship

Relationship: Students.country_id → Countries.country_id

The tables are joined via country_id. In the Students table, country_id is a Foreign Key referencing country_id in the Countries table. This represents a One-to-Many (1:N) relationship: each country contains approximately 9,800 to 10,200 sampled students, while each student resides in exactly one country.

[Students Table] [Countries Table] • student_id (PK) • country_id (PK) • country_id (FK) ─────────────────────────────► • country_name • age • development_level • gender • poverty_rate_percent • device_access • internet_infrastructure_index • social_media_hours • study_hours_per_week • productivity_score • digital_addiction_score • usage_level

💻 SQL Table Creation Statements (CREATE TABLE)

-- Table 1: Create Countries Reference Table CREATE TABLE countries ( country_id INT PRIMARY KEY, country_name VARCHAR(60) NOT NULL UNIQUE, development_level VARCHAR(30) NOT NULL, poverty_rate_percent DECIMAL(5,2), internet_infrastructure_index DECIMAL(5,2) ); -- Table 2: Create Students Main Entity Table with Foreign Key CREATE TABLE students ( student_id INT PRIMARY KEY, country_id INT, age INT, gender VARCHAR(15), device_access VARCHAR(30), social_media_hours DECIMAL(4,2), study_hours_per_week DECIMAL(5,2), productivity_score DECIMAL(4,2), digital_addiction_score DECIMAL(5,2), brain_rot_index DECIMAL(5,2), academic_risk_score DECIMAL(5,2), usage_level VARCHAR(20), FOREIGN KEY (country_id) REFERENCES countries(country_id) );

Query 3: High-Risk Digital Distraction Cohort

WHERE • Multiple Conditions (AND)
Purpose: Identify students who simultaneously experience heavy daily screen time (> 5.0 hrs), severe digital addiction (> 20.0), and low academic motivation (< 4.0 out of 10).

Explanation: Combines multiple boolean conditions using AND to isolate students simultaneously experiencing behavioral addiction, time displacement, and academic disengagement.

Query 5: Global Sample Descriptive Aggregation

COUNT / AVG / MIN / MAX
Purpose: Calculate overall descriptive statistics across the entire global sample of 500,000 students, including respondent count, average hours, and range bounds.
SELECT COUNT(*) AS total_students, ROUND(AVG(social_media_hours), 2) AS avg_screen_time_hours, ROUND(AVG(study_hours_per_week), 2) AS avg_weekly_study_hours, ROUND(AVG(productivity_score), 2) AS avg_productivity_score, MIN(social_media_hours) AS min_screen_time, MAX(social_media_hours) AS max_screen_time FROM students;
Explanation: Employs aggregate functions (COUNT, AVG, MIN, MAX) combined with ROUND() to summarize the 500k dataset in a single summary record.

Query 6: Study Duration & Productivity by Usage Tier (Source for Graph 2)

GROUP BY Comparison
Purpose: Group students by daily usage tier (Low, Moderate, High) to count participants per tier and compare average study hours, productivity, and addiction scores.
SELECT usage_level, COUNT(*) AS student_count, ROUND(AVG(study_hours_per_week), 2) AS avg_study_hours, ROUND(AVG(productivity_score), 2) AS avg_productivity, ROUND(AVG(digital_addiction_score), 2) AS avg_addiction FROM students GROUP BY usage_level ORDER BY student_count DESC;
Explanation: The GROUP BY clause partitions rows into the three discrete usage categories, aggregating student counts and average study hours for each group.

Query 7: High-Consumption National Cohort Screening

HAVING Clause
Purpose: Group participants by nation and apply the HAVING clause to identify countries where average social media screen time exceeds 3.40 hours with cohort size ≥ 9,500.
SELECT c.country_name, COUNT(s.student_id) AS student_count, ROUND(AVG(s.social_media_hours), 2) AS avg_screen_time, ROUND(AVG(s.study_hours_per_week), 2) AS avg_study_time FROM students s JOIN countries c ON s.country_id = c.country_id GROUP BY c.country_name HAVING COUNT(s.student_id) >= 9500 AND AVG(s.social_media_hours) > 3.40 ORDER BY avg_screen_time DESC;
Explanation: While WHERE filters individual rows before grouping, HAVING filters aggregated groups after the GROUP BY operation (here, retaining only countries with COUNT(*) ≥ 9500 and AVG(screen_time) > 3.40). Exactly 24 countries qualify.

Query 8: Multi-Table Development Tier Aggregation (Source for Graph 1)

JOIN Between Two Tables
Purpose: Perform an INNER JOIN linking Students with Countries to compare digital habits and academic productivity across economic development classifications.
SELECT c.development_level, COUNT(s.student_id) AS total_students, ROUND(AVG(s.social_media_hours), 2) AS avg_social_media_hours, ROUND(AVG(s.study_hours_per_week), 2) AS avg_weekly_study_hours, ROUND(AVG(s.productivity_score), 2) AS avg_productivity_score FROM students s INNER JOIN countries c ON s.country_id = c.country_id GROUP BY c.development_level ORDER BY total_students DESC;
Explanation: Combines rows from students and countries where s.country_id = c.country_id, allowing macro-economic country indicators to be combined with individual student behaviors.

🔬 Research Connection

Relational SQL operations provide an efficient, transparent, and reproducible methodology to validate our core research hypotheses on 500,000 respondents:

  • SQL Filtering (WHERE & Multiple Conditions): Allows us to isolate high-risk behavioral cohorts. In Query 3, combining multiple conditions identified that 10,224 students (2.04%) concurrently experience > 5.0 hours of daily screen time, elevated digital addiction (> 20.0), and low academic motivation (< 4.0), demonstrating active cognitive distraction and supporting Hypothesis 2.
  • SQL Grouping & Aggregation (GROUP BY, COUNT, AVG): Enables empirical comparisons across usage tiers. In Query 6, aggregating average study hours reveals a continuous drop from 23.77 hours/week for Low users down to 22.71 hours/week for Moderate users and 21.23 hours/week for High users—demonstrating a 2.54-hour weekly study deficit (over 130 hours across a school year) and validating the time-displacement principle (supporting Hypothesis 1).
  • Relational Table JOINs: Links individual student behavioral metrics with national socio-economic indicators (Query 8), proving that developed nations exhibit higher average screen time (4.03 hrs/day) than developing (3.33 hrs) and underdeveloped nations (2.69 hrs), while academic productivity is inversely impacted.
